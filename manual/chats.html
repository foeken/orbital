    Where your chats live

    Chats are documents like any other, so the Library lists them next to your tasks and notes. Filter the Library to Chats with the type pill to see only them, newest first. A meeting's private AI chat ("Private AI chat for …") shows up here too, while the chats Tana starts for its own background work stay out.

    Toggle MCP chats

    Every time another app asks Tana something through MCP, it leaves a chat whose title starts with "MCP:". To keep them out of the way, run ⌘K Toggle MCP chats (under Settings; its hint says Shown or Hidden). It hides them from every list and every search at once, and the setting follows you to your other Macs. A chat you open by its link still opens.

    ⌘S→ type #chat to find a chat by its title.

    A chat reads as a conversation

    Open a chat and it starts at its newest message, with the caret in the message box. It keeps following new messages as long as you stay near the bottom. Scroll up to read older ones, and the page stays where you left it.

    1. Who can see it "Only you can see this chat", or the faces of the people in it. The Add participants link opens the sharing page.
    2. Your messages In a light blue bubble on the right. Hover over one to see when it was sent.
    3. Thought for 14 seconds How long Tana worked on the answer below it.
    4. Who answered In bold over each run of replies: Tana AI, a colleague, or ChatGPT.
    5. References Nodes a message mentions or attaches, as links that open them.
    6. Proposals What Tana suggested creating or changing, as cards.
    7. The message box It stays at the bottom however long the chat gets.

    Tana's answers keep their shape: headings, bullets, numbered lists, quotes and code. Messages themselves cannot be edited, and anything you cannot write to says so in the box: You can read this chat but not write in it.

    Thinking, and what Tana did

    While Tana works on an answer, a grey line over it shimmers Thinking.... When the answer is done, the line says how long it took: Thought for 14 seconds, "2 minutes", or Finished thinking when the time is not known. The line shows only on answers where Tana used its tools.

    When Tana handed part of the work to a helper, the line gets a chevron. Click it to fold out a link to that helper's own chat. If an answer failed, the reason sits in the same grey place: Error: ….

    Proposals inside a chat

    When Tana suggests a new document, a change to one, or an action in another system, the suggestion arrives as a card in its answer. The card names the thing, what kind of proposal it is and where it stands: awaiting your approval, approved or rejected.

    While a card waits, it has the same approve and reject buttons as the Proposals page. Click the card itself to open what was proposed; ⌘-click opens it in a new tab. A change to an existing document is merged by Tana itself, so its approve button stays greyed with approve in Tana as the reason. All your pending proposals, across chats, are on the Proposals page: see Timeline, notifications & proposals.

    Write a message

    Type in the box and press ↩ to send. ⇧↩ starts a new line, and esc leaves the box. Three dots show where Tana's answer will appear, and the answer then streams in as Tana writes it.

    Whatever you type and do not send stays in that chat's box while the window is open. If a message cannot be sent, your words go back into the box.

    @ links and mentions

    Type @ to link a node. Tana comes first: mentioning it asks Tana to answer, even in a chat with other people. ChatGPT comes next (see below), then your recent pages and search results. Pasting a Tana link turns it into the node's chip as well, and so does dragging a row onto the chat: drop it anywhere on the conversation, or on a chat in the sidebar, and its chip lands in the message box.

    / runs a skill

    Typed as the very first character, / opens the workspace's skills. The one you pick sits as a pill in front of your text and goes with the message for Tana to run. ⌫ at the start of the text, or a click on the pill, removes it.

    Add to chat …

    To talk about something you are looking at, run ⌘K Add to chat … on it (or on a selection: "Add 3 items to chat …"). Pick New chat or one of your chats, and the items land at the start of that chat's message box, ready for your question. Dragging rows onto a chat's message box does the same.

    In a chat: type @ →↩ on Tana → type your question →↩

    To Tana, or to the chat ⇥

    Each message goes one of two ways, and the empty box tells you which:

    • Ask Tana · @ links · / runs a skill: Tana is asked to answer.
    • Message the chat · @ links · Tab: to Tana: the message is for the people in the chat, and Tana stays quiet.

    A chat starts in the mode it uses by itself: a chat that is yours alone asks Tana, and a chat with other people in it messages them. Press ⇥ in the empty box to switch. Mentioning @Tana asks it to answer in either mode. While a skill is attached, the message stays in To Tana, since the skill is for Tana to run.

    When Tana asks you something

    Sometimes Tana needs an answer before it goes on. Its message then reads Waiting for your input, and a question card takes the message box's place, one question at a time ("2 of 2").

    • ↑ ↓ move through the options; 1–9 pick one directly.
    • Space ticks an option where it says Select all that apply.
    • The last row, No, and tell Tana what to do differently, takes your own words.
    • ↩ is Continue, and Submit on the last question. With nothing picked, it takes the highlighted option.
    • ← → step between questions.
    • esc is Dismiss: Tana goes on with sensible defaults.

    New chats, and bringing people in

    New chat and New chat with …

    ⌘K New chat starts a chat with Tana that only you can see, and opens it. It is called "New chat" until Tana names it after its first answer. New chat with … lists your colleagues. Pick one, and the new chat is shared with just the two of you.

    Invite to chat…

    On an open chat, ⌘K Invite to chat… lists the workspace's members. The one you pick can read and write in the chat from then on. A line in the middle of the chat says so: "Sam Okafor was added to the chat." Once there are two of you, Tana answers only when mentioned, and the box moves to To the chat.

    A chat that takes its audience from a meeting or a space cannot be narrowed to a list of people, so Orbital refuses the invite there and says why.

    Rename

    ⌘K Rename on a chat you can edit lets you type a new title. The messages themselves stay as they are.

    Select and delete messages ⇧⌘⌫

    A chat can be read from the keyboard. ↑ at the very start of the message box selects the newest message, with a ring around it. ↑ and ↓ walk through the messages, and ↓ past the last one, or esc, takes you back to the box.

    With one of your own messages selected, ⇧⌘⌫ or ⌘K Delete message removes it from the chat, for everyone in it. You can delete only your own messages, never Tana's or a colleague's.

    With a message selected, ⌘K opens with that message's own rows at the top, under Message.

    Ask @ChatGPT, privately

    Pick ChatGPT after @ and your question goes to a ChatGPT task on this Mac instead of to Tana. ChatGPT reads the conversation and answers you alone. Nothing of it is written to Tana, so nobody else in the chat sees the question or the answer.

    The chat
    Tana and everyone in it
    Draft the memo?
    This Mac
    Only you
    @ChatGPT what changed?
    Draft the memo? @ChatGPT what changed?

    Your question shows in a grey bubble among the messages, marked with a crossed-out cloud: "Only visible for you, on this device. Never saved to Tana." ChatGPT's answer comes under it in grey, with the dots until it arrives. Questions and answers are kept on this Mac, so they are still there after a restart.

    • Add to message on ChatGPT's name line (or ↩ on the selected answer, or ⌘K Add ChatGPT’s answer to message) puts the answer in your message box, to send as your own words.
    • Click the name line, or run ⌘K Open ChatGPT task, to open the task in the ChatGPT app.
    • Delete message on the question removes it and its answer from this Mac.

    ChatGPT needs to be set up on this Mac first. AI & agents covers ChatGPT and handing whole nodes to an agent.

    Chat with ChatGPT

    A chat can also be with ChatGPT itself. ⌘K New chat with ChatGPT opens an empty chat, and your first message starts it. ChatGPT answers on the page the way Tana does, its commands folded into the thinking line. The conversation is a ChatGPT thread on this Mac, so it is in the ChatGPT app as well, where you can carry on. The conversation is never written to Tana: Orbital keeps only the chat's title and where it is, in your own Orbital settings, which only you can see, so your other Macs can list it.

    • The sidebar lists your newest chats with ChatGPT under Agent chats, and ⌘K Agent chats opens all of them, newest first. One started on another Mac is listed too, and opens on that Mac. Though they are not in Tana, ⌘K and ⌘S find them by their title, and your Dot's by its name.
    • The line at the top says it is private, and which model and reasoning effort ChatGPT answers with.
    • Your Dot, once linked (AI & agents), has a chat of its own at the top of the list. That chat goes when you unlink the Dot, and cannot be renamed or deleted before.
    • Rename chat …, in ⌘K or as Rename when you right-click its tab, renames it here and in the ChatGPT app. Set icon gives it a glyph of its own.
    • ⌘K Open in ChatGPT shows the chat in the ChatGPT app, Stop ChatGPT stops an answer being written, and Delete chat takes it off the list. It stays in the ChatGPT app.
    • ChatGPT works in a folder of its own, ~/.orbital/chats, and does not stop to ask you for approval: its own reviewer decides. When the ChatGPT app has the chat open, your message is queued there and answered in the app.

    Your Dot's chat goes through the Orbital MCP server

    A chat with ChatGPT goes from this Mac straight to OpenAI, as it does in the ChatGPT app: it never passes through us. Your Dot is different: everything you write to it, and every answer it gives, goes through the Orbital MCP server it is linked on. That is orbital.md, Orbital's relay, run by us, unless your workspace runs its own Orbital MCP server, which keeps all of it within your workspace. The chat says which, at the top and in the message box.

    The server passes each message on as it comes and keeps nothing of it. It keeps an answer only until Orbital collects it, a few seconds while you wait, and a week at most if this Mac is off. The conversation itself is kept on this Mac, and nothing of it goes to Tana.
