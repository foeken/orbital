    A meeting opens at its write-up

    After a meeting, Tana writes it up: a short summary, the key takeaways and the decisions, in a document titled after the meeting. Orbital treats that document as the meeting's page. Click a meeting anywhere, on the Timeline, in a search, in a mention or in your pins, and Orbital opens its write-up straight away.

    You read and edit the write-up like any other page. The meeting's own facts, who can see it and who was invited, sit under the title. Everything else Tana keeps about the meeting (what was pinned to it, the tasks that came out of it, notes written in it) shows in the Graph pane beside it.

    1. Title The write-up's title, which Tana gives it with the write-up.
    2. Visible to Who can read the meeting. Click it to change who can see it.
    3. Attendees Who was invited, one per line: colleagues as links, guests by name.
    4. Summary Tana's few lines on what the meeting was about.
    5. Key takeaways The write-up itself, with the decisions under it. Edit it like any note.
    6. Pinned Documents and chats pinned to the meeting.
    7. Outcomes Tasks that came out of the meeting.
    8. References Other notes written in the meeting.
    A meeting on Tana's side is a calendar event with no content of its own. Its write-up is the document whose title is the meeting's "The one where …" line, so a meeting that has not been written up yet opens on its empty event.
    ⌘K→ type Timeline →↩, then click any meeting under Upcoming meetings.

    What the meeting page tells you

    Attendees

    Attendees lists the people on the invitation. Colleagues are links to their profile; anyone else shows the name or address the calendar gave. Rooms and equipment are left out. After five people the list stops at And 3 more. Click it, or press ↩ on it, to see everyone.

    An invitation says who was asked, not who came. For that, see the meeting you are in now.

    Join call

    When the meeting has a video link, ⌘K on its page offers Join call, with the link as its hint. It opens the call in your browser. Orbital takes the first web link in the meeting's location, or else the call link Tana keeps for the meeting.

    Open in Tana opens the same meeting in Tana's web app.

    The Graph pane opens with ⌘K Show graph, or the graph button at the top right of the page. On a meeting it lists Pinned, Outcomes, Proposals (what Tana's AI proposed in the meeting's chats), References, where the meeting is mentioned, and its Changes. See Windows, panes & the Graph.

    Change the time, place and people

    If you organise a meeting, you can change it from ⌘K on its page. These rows appear under Current node, each with what it would change as its hint: Change time … shows when it is now, Change location … where, Add attendee … how many people are on it, and Edit meeting details takes the time in your own words. Tana sends the change on to your calendar.

    On a meeting someone else organises, these rows are not there, because Tana would refuse the change. Change time … and Edit meeting details are also missing on an all-day meeting, which Tana cannot move to a clock time. A meeting you make in Orbital, from Create new … or “/”, asks for its name and then shows when it will be before anything is made: now, for 30 minutes, unless you type another time (the When page). Made from Create new …, the meeting opens; from “/”, the row becomes a reference to it.

    Change time …

    Type a day, a time, or both. The row under the field shows exactly the time ↩ will set, so you see it before anything changes.

    • 14:00 keeps the day and the length
    • tomorrow 9:30 moves both
    • fri 10:00-11:30 sets the start and the end
    • tomorrow keeps the time of day

    Days are read the way Pin to date … reads them: weekdays, "in 3 days", "next week", dates.

    Edit meeting details

    One field: say when, the way you would say it. tomorrow from 3-5, an hour later or thursday at 10 for 45 minutes. Press ↩ and the AI reads the words; while it does, the row thinks. What it read is shown as a row beside the meeting's time now, marked read by AI, and only pressing that row changes the meeting. Words typed after the reading are read again.

    • A day you leave out is today. A bare 1 to 6 is the afternoon, so 3-5 is 15:00 to 17:00; a time you spell out, like 03:00 or midnight, is kept.
    • A length you leave out is the meeting's own.
    • Times are yours: on your Mac's clock. A meeting kept in another time zone says so under the row, and a zone you name, like 9am New York time, is used as named.
    • When the words are not enough, the AI asks back in the same field ("Which day next week?").

    The AI reads only the time. It cannot add people, change the place or send anything. It is your Quick AI (⌘K Choose models), and it needs a ChatGPT sign-in or an API key (AI and agents).

    Change location …

    Type a room, an address or a link, and press ↩ on Set location to "…". With the field empty, the page shows the current location and offers Remove location.

    Add attendee …

    The page lists the people you meet with most, then your colleagues. Whoever is already invited is left out. Type to narrow the list, or type a full email address to add someone from outside. The page stays open, so you can add several people in a row.

    The field says it: New attendees may get a calendar invite. If your calendar can send one, it will.

    On a meeting you organise: ⌘K→ type time →↩→ type tomorrow 9:30 →↩

    Put things on the table: pin to a meeting

    Pin to current meeting

    In a call right now? ⌘K Pin to current meeting pins the page you are on to the meeting you have joined. Its hint names that meeting, says Checking… while Orbital asks Tana, or says No active meeting when you are in no call.

    Pin to meeting …

    Pin to meeting … lets you pick any of your meetings from a week back to a week ahead. The next one is at the top, then the rest still to come, then those that are over. Type to narrow the list, and press ↩ to pin.

    You can also drag a row onto a meeting's row, on the Timeline or in any list. The meeting's row lights up while you hover, and dropping pins the row to it.

    To take a pin off again, use ⌘K Edit pins, which lists everywhere the page is pinned, meetings included. Pins & dates has the rest.

    On any task: ⌘K→ type pin to meeting →↩→ pick a meeting →↩

    The meeting you are in now

    The top of the Timeline is about today. Under Upcoming meetings are the meetings still to come, earliest first, each with its time and faces for who else is on it. The Tana glyph after a title (Join in Tana) opens that meeting in Tana, where you join the call. Above them, a line says how much free time you have before the next one.

    On the record

    Once a meeting starts, it moves into the Timeline's history at its start time, with its length and who else is in it. While somebody is in its call and Tana is transcribing or recording it, its marker turns blue and a blue ring pulses out of it. The ring goes when the last person leaves or the call goes off the record.

    Being in the call is also what Pin to current meeting uses to know which meeting you mean.

    A meeting that is over and has no write-up is drawn faint on the Timeline: there is nothing to read yet.

    Tasks that came from a meeting

    When Tana's AI takes tasks out of a meeting, it files them under that meeting. Those are the meeting's Outcomes, and each one keeps a way back to where it came from.

    The meeting glyph on a row

    In any list, such a task has a small grey calendar glyph among its facts. Hover over it to read which meeting it came from, for example "From Leadership sync · Wed 30 Sep". Click it to open that meeting. Your caret stays in the row.

    The meeting button in the header

    On the task's own page, a calendar button joins the buttons at the top right. It shows while your pointer is over the page, and its tooltip names the meeting. Click it to open the meeting's write-up. The button is never on the write-up itself, since that page is the meeting.

    Open a task from a meeting → move the pointer over the page → click the calendar button at the top right
