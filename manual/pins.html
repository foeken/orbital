    Three places to pin

    Every pin row lives in ⌘K, under Current node. Type pin with the caret in a node, or on its page, and you see them all.

    Yours

    Sidebar

    Keeps a node in Tana's sidebar, on every device you use.

     Yours

    A day

    Puts the node on today, tomorrow or any date, as a plan for that day.

     Shared

    A meeting

    Hangs the node on a meeting, where everyone in it can see it.

    Sidebar and day pins are personal: nobody else sees them, and you can pin a node you can only read. A pin on a meeting is part of that meeting, so it is shared with its people.

    Pinning never moves or copies the node. It stays where it lives, and unpinning only takes away the pointer.

    Pin to the sidebar

    The sidebar is Tana's own list of your favourite places, the same one you see at home.tana.inc, and every Orbital window shows it on its left. At the top are Search (⌘S), Home, Today and Agent chats (Chat with Codex). Under them come the nodes you pinned: the ones in no section under Pinned, then each of your sections. Click a pin to open it in the pane you are working in; the one on screen is highlighted. Click a section's name to fold it, and it shows how many pins it holds.

    To pin the node you are on, open ⌘K and choose Pin to sidebar …. It asks where: Pinned, one of your sections, or a new section, which you name by typing. A node already in the sidebar moves to the section you pick. Edit pins offers the same row.

    Drag the sidebar's edge to make it wider or narrower. Let go of it narrow and it keeps only its icons, each named when you point at it; it never disappears. Collapse sidebar and Expand sidebar in ⌘K, or ⌃⌘S, switch between the two.

    To take a pin out of the sidebar, right-click it there and choose Remove pin. The node itself stays where it is.

    A pinned node carries a small tack among its grey facts, wherever it is listed. Click the tack to open Edit pins for it.

    Pin to a day

    Pin to today and Pin to tomorrow put the node on that day. Once it is there, the row reads Unpin from today and takes it off again, so one recorded key does both. When the node is pinned to that day for everyone, unpinning mutes it for you alone.

    Pin to date …

    For any other day, choose Pin to date … and type the day in words. The page shows the date it read before you press ↩, so you always see what you are about to pin. It understands:

    • today, tomorrow, a weekday such as fri (the next one)
    • in 3 days, 2w, 1 month, next week (the coming Monday)
    • 12 oct, 12/10 (day first), 2026-10-12; a date without a year is the next time it comes round

    Where day pins show up

    Tasks pinned to today, or to an earlier day while still open, fill Today's Tasks at the top of the Timeline. A task list grouped by Responsibility gathers them in a Pinned section, each with its days on the grey line: "Pinned to Today · 2026-09-30".

    ⌘K→ type pin to date →↩→ type next week →↩

    Edit pins

    ⌘K Edit pins shows every place the node is pinned: the sidebar, each day, each meeting. Its hint in ⌘K already says it, such as "Sidebar, 1 date", or "Not pinned".

    ↩ on a pin takes it off, and the page stays open for the next one. Under Pin it are the places it is not pinned yet: Pin to sidebar …, Pin to today, Pin to tomorrow and Pin to meeting …. Typing narrows both halves; with nothing left the page says "No pin matches" or "Not pinned anywhere". Type sidebar and ↩ pins it there: the highlight starts on the row your words found, not on that grey line.

    Click the tack on a row →↩ to unpin it from the first place listed.

    Pin to a meeting

    A meeting has pinned items of its own: the documents and tasks it is about. Two rows add to them.

    • Pin to current meeting pins the node on the meeting you have joined right now. Its hint names the meeting, or says "No active meeting" and stays grey.
    • Pin to meeting … lets you choose: your meetings from a week back to a week ahead, the next one first. Type to narrow, ↩ pins.

    The pinned node then shows on the meeting's page, in the Pinned section of its Graph pane, for everyone in the meeting. Spaces keep pins the same way. Taking one off goes through Edit pins and needs permission to change that meeting. Meetings tells the rest.

    Today and This week

    Besides pins, Orbital keeps a page per day and per week, the way many people keep a daily note. ⌘K Today, or ⌃⇧D, opens today's page, titled with the date (2026-09-30). This week opens the week's page, such as "Week 40 (2026)". Each is made the first time you ask for it.

    Add to Today

    With the caret on a node, ⌘K Add to Today writes a line into today's page that points at it. Add to Tomorrow and Add to This Week do the same for those pages. With rows selected they read Add 3 items to Today.

    The tasks on today's page count as Today's Tasks on the Timeline, done ones included.

    Dates in your notes

    A date can live in the text itself. Type @ and then a day in words, like friday or 12 oct. Words that read as a day put that date first in the list; ↩ writes it as a date chip, such as "Oct 2, 2026". It reads the same words as Pin to date ….

    A date chip is a link like any other mention; Writing in the outline covers @ in full.
